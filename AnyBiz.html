About

AnyBiz is a B2B platform that hires out AI sales agents. They find prospects, write to them by email and LinkedIn, call them, keep the brand visible with posts, and book meetings into the calendar.

Problem

Outreach eats a sales team's week: finding the right people, writing to each of them, following up, calling, and keeping the CRM current. AI can do all of it, but the people who own a company's reputation are afraid to let it speak for them.

Solution

AI agents that work across email, LinkedIn and phone under instructions the customer writes: who to reach, in which language and tone, with which call to action and signature. The customer sees an example before anything goes out, and every result comes back as insights.

Why users stay

Integrations are a big part of why many users love AnyBiz: their CRM, calendar, automation and messaging tools work from one place, and leads, meetings and replies stay in sync on their own.

I designed every integration as one pattern, so a new one never needs a new idea: a status (Connected, Incomplete, Inactive, Coming soon), one button to connect, the docs a click away, and early access for what is next.

ResearchTested before it was built

Every feature met our users before it met the developers

I built dynamic prototypes of the AI tools and ran user interviews on them before anything went into development. People worked through real tasks on the prototype, so we saw how they behaved, not what they promised. It made the product better and taught us who our users are. Three outcomes kept coming back.

  1. 01

    Sharpened

    Most ideas survived, but not as drawn. Where people hesitated, misread a label or expected a different next step, the fix went into the design before a line of code was written.

  2. 02

    Dropped

    Some features never reached the backlog. When interviews showed that our audience did not need them, we stopped them at the prototype and saved the team weeks of building.

  3. 03

    Replaced

    Sometimes the interviews pointed to a different need. We set the planned feature aside and designed the one that really strengthened the product.

Key insightTrust is designed

Users did not want to hand their company to AI. They wanted to instruct it.

The interviews showed it again and again: people did not trust AI agents and were afraid to let one speak for their company. So we did not ask them to trust a black box. We gave them clear instructions to set for the agent, channel by channel: the persona, the language, the tone of voice, the call to action, the signature, message guidelines and case studies.

Before anything goes out, the agent writes an example; the customer can regenerate it, or save it and train the AI. With the rules in their hands, customers trust the agent with the outreach of their company.

BehaviourWhat people do, not what they say

Recordings show what people do when nobody is asking

Interviews tell you what people think. Session recordings show what they actually do.

I watched real sessions to find the moments that need attention: where people hesitate, click something that does nothing, click again in frustration, or leave a flow half done. Each moment became a design fix, a question for the next interview, or a hypothesis to test.
