About

Sport Guardian (Idrettsfadder in Norwegian) is a crowdfunding solution from Supporter.no. A club starts a campaign, and private people and companies become its sports guardians: they give once or every month, and the money covers fees, gear and travel for children whose families cannot pay.

Problem

Sport costs money, and the cost is what makes children quit first. 185,000 Norwegians cancelled a sports club membership last year, and the drop was largest among children and young people. Clubs had no simple way to ask their community for help.

Solution

A campaign page that shows the countdown, the amount raised and the people behind it, and a donation that takes four choices: private person or company, once or recurring, the amount, then card or Vipps. Behind it, a builder that lets a club publish a campaign without help, and an admin panel with donors, refunds and statistics.

Product

From the donor to the club's admin

Card or Vipps

Donors pay by card, or with Vipps, the mobile payment app used only in the Nordic countries: in Norway most people pay with it in one tap, and donors from anywhere else pay by card.

What this chart is

A Sankey diagram. Each band is as wide as the amount it carries, so you can follow the money from who gives, through how often they give, to the total.

When to use it: when a whole splits and merges across stages and the question is where the volume goes, as with a budget, the money in a campaign, or the paths people take through a product. With more than about a dozen nodes, or when exact values must be compared, a table or a bar chart reads better.

Other charts, and when to use them

  • Bar chartCompares amounts across categories, such as money raised per club or per campaign.
  • Line chartShows a trend over time, such as money raised day by day.
  • Area chartA line chart with the volume filled in: for a total that builds up over time.
  • Stacked bar chartParts of a whole, compared across groups, such as one-time and recurring gifts per campaign.
  • Radar chartCompares several measures of one thing at once, such as how a club does on donors, gifts, reach and repeat giving against the average; reads best with five to eight measures.
  • Donut or progress ringOne share of a whole, or progress to a goal; reads best with one to three parts.
  • Funnel chartHow many people drop off between the steps of a flow, from the campaign page to the payment.
  • HeatmapPatterns across two dimensions, such as donations by weekday and hour.
  • Scatter plotHow two measures relate, such as the size of a gift and how often the donor gives.
