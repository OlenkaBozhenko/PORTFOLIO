# Senior portfolio expectations and 10 live examples (2026-09-24)

URLs checked on 2026-09-24 unless noted. Time-per-portfolio and AI statistics are practitioner estimates from blog posts, not studies.
Not used as primary sources: Lenny's Newsletter (no portfolio post found), Bestfolios Medium (403).

## A. What recruiters and hiring managers want

### Number of cases, structure, length
- 3–5 cases, quality over quantity — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-design-portfolios/); "three to five focused case studies, no more" — [Tom Scott](https://verifiedinsider.substack.com/p/faq-design-hiring-in-2026); 2–3 full-journey, shipped — [Figma blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/portfolio/); 3–4 — [Leslie Yang](https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in).
- **NN/g structure** (survey of 204 hiring managers): problem / hypothesis → role and team → how solutions were developed → how they solved it → challenges and rejected concepts → user and business impact → lessons. Quotes: "I'm curious to know what isn't in the design and why"; "Show me how you started with an opportunity and produced real value."
- Figma recruiter Korin Harris ([blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/product-design-portfolio-tips-from-a-figma-recruiter/)): name the context (people, timeline, constraints, goals); before and after; more images than text.
- Time: ~10 s on the homepage, 2–3 min per case if it earns it ([Emily Backes](https://emilybackes.design/post/what-i-actually-look-at-in-a-portfolio-review)); < 3 min per portfolio ([Holysheva, DOU](https://dou.ua/lenta/columns/how-to-make-design-portfolio/)); 10–15 s on the homepage, "AI screens 78% of applications" — unsourced ([Fountain Institute](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/senior-product-designers-hired)).
- Length: strong senior cases run ~1,500–2,000 words; "reams of text" is red flag #1 ([Tom Scott, 8 Red Flags](https://verifiedinsider.substack.com/p/8-red-flags-in-design-portfolios)). Visual / text ≈ 40/60 for UX, 50/50 for UI (Holysheva).

### Seniority signals
- Leslie Yang ([Part 2](https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in-d25)): **initiative** (co-write the brief with the PM), **product thinking** (problem ≠ solution), **scope** (own an app, surface or vertical; coordinate teams), **innovation** (one boundary-pushing project). Initiative and product thinking rank highest.
- Tom Scott red flag #8: title (Senior / Staff / Principal) doesn't match the scope shown. Leaders: show cross-team influence with metrics.
- Fountain Institute: "trade-offs demonstrated, not processes"; decisions under uncertainty.
- Holysheva: seniors "always begin with a good story".

### Homepage, about, CV
- Losing a reviewer fastest: "a homepage that makes me guess what you actually do" (Backes).
- Projects one click away, "equally sized, thoughtfully crafted rectangles" (Figma recruiter).
- Nav: About, Cases, Contact; "should not require explanations" (DOU).
- A Dribbble / Behance-only gallery gets skipped — authorship unclear (Leslie Yang).

### NDA / password-protected work
- Show process artefacts, redact / blur, or rebuild generically (NN/g).
- "Password-protected or coming soon projects" is a red flag ([Designfolio](https://designfolio.substack.com/p/portfolio-tips-ux)).
- Put the password in the application; never a bare "can't share due to NDA" slide ([UX Playbook FAQ](https://uxplaybook.org/faq/how-to-add-nda-projects-to-portfolio)).
- Cautionary: simonpan.com is entirely password-walled (HTTP 401); aleksitappura.com says only "Portfolio available on request."

### AI, prototypes, code
- Tom Scott 2026: "Show AI fluency. This means more than mentioning tools" — Cursor / Claude prototypes, interactions not static screens. "No AI signals" = red flag #3.
- Fountain Institute: portfolio is "only the trailer"; shipped, traceable live work beats concept redesigns; 73% AI-skills figure unsourced.

### Red flags (combined: Tom Scott, Backes, Figma, Designfolio)
Unclear ownership ("we" everywhere) · process theatre · no shipped products · template more impressive than the work · too much text · weak craft at first glance · heavy animations · typos, broken links, no HTTPS, uncompressed images, poor mobile layout · sameness.

### Mobile, performance, accessibility
- Poor mobile responsiveness and uncompressed images are red flags (Backes); mobile optimisation, working links, HTTPS (Designfolio).
- [The Crit, Nikki Kipple](https://thecrit.co/resources/accessibility-for-portfolios): WCAG 2.2 AA baseline — one H1, semantic heading order, keyboard access, visible focus, 4.5:1 contrast, semantic HTML, captions and alt text; test real tasks (find your role, open a project, contact you).
