## Guidance from reviewers

- Emily Backes, "What I actually look at in a portfolio review": https://emilybackes.design/post/what-i-actually-look-at-in-a-portfolio-review. Name your own contribution in the first sentence of a section ("I moderated five sessions", not "we ran testing"); lead with the number.
- Lucas Rappart, "7 product designer portfolio mistakes that block senior roles": https://lucasrappart.com/blog/portfolio-mistakes-senior. Three decisions with reasons; what you led, paired on, handed off; one rejected direction; a three-sentence reflection.
- Jeff Humble, Fountain Institute, "What actually gets senior product designers hired in 2026": https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/senior-product-designers-hired. "Show your trade-offs, not your process"; 500–800 words a case.

## The pattern

1. At the top: an at-a-glance block with role, team, timeline and the headline result.
2. "My role" in the first person: what she did herself.
3. Findings as evidence (numbers, quotes).
4. Decisions as headings, each with the alternative rejected and the trade-off.
5. What changed: numbers, or plain behavioural evidence, said honestly when there are none.
6. A short "Looking back" at the end.
